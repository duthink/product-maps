# Product Map — Shopfloor Production Visibility

This Product Map documents how the operations team navigated
an initiative to improve production visibility on the shopfloor.

The goal was not automation for its own sake,
but reducing delays, confusion, and reactive firefighting.

---

## 1. What are we trying to move from → to?
(M — Mark)

**From**
- Manual production tracking on paper and whiteboards
- Delayed visibility into output and downtime
- Frequent last-minute escalations

**To**
- Near real-time visibility of production status
- Fewer surprises at shift handovers
- Earlier identification of bottlenecks

**Who experiences this change the most**
- Shift supervisors
- Production planners
- Line operators during handovers

---

## 2. What do we believe — and what do we know?
(M → A)

**What we believed to be true**
- Operators would enter data if given a digital system
- Real-time dashboards would improve decision-making
- Visibility would automatically reduce downtime

**What evidence supported this**
- Managers frequently requested status updates
- Downtime reasons were discussed retrospectively
- Planning decisions were often based on yesterday’s data

**What felt uncertain or risky**
- Whether operators had time or incentive to enter data
- Whether dashboards would be checked during shifts
- Resistance to “monitoring” tools

---

## 3. What does reality push back on?
(A — Align)

**How work happens today**
- Operators prioritize keeping machines running
- Data is filled in after the fact, often from memory
- Issues are communicated verbally, not systematically

**Constraints that won’t bend**
- Operators cannot stop machines to update systems
- Shift changes are rushed
- Trust between floor and management is fragile

**What changed after looking at reality**
- Manual data entry during production is unrealistic
- Visibility must emerge from existing actions, not extra work
- Adoption matters more than precision

---

## 4. What is the next meaningful step?
(P — Proceed)

**Purpose of the next milestone**
- Improve visibility at shift handover without disrupting production

**What we deliberately did not do**
- Full MES implementation
- Per-minute tracking of all machines
- Performance dashboards tied to individual operators

**Smallest step that proves direction**
- Capture only downtime events using simple buttons
- Auto-generate a shift summary visible to the next shift
- Review data only at the supervisor level

**How we’ll know this worked**
- Fewer clarification calls during shift changes
- Faster response to repeated downtime causes
- Operators do not resist the system

---

## 5. How will we know if we’re wrong?
(S — Sync)

**Signals we’ll monitor**
- Frequency of downtime reporting
- Usage of shift summaries
- Operator and supervisor feedback

**Review cadence**
- Weekly ops review for first month

**Reroute triggers**
- Operators bypass the system
- Data accuracy drops sharply
- Trust issues surface in feedback

---

## Notes / Revisions

- 2025-03-01 — Reduced scope from real-time tracking to shift-level visibility  
  Reason: shopfloor observations showed data entry disrupted flow and trust
