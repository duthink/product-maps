# Product Map — Checkout Conversion Drop (D2C E-commerce)

This Product Map documents how the team navigated
a sudden drop in checkout conversion on a D2C website.

Traffic remained stable.
Product quality was unchanged.
Revenue declined.

---

## 1. What are we trying to move from → to?
(M — Mark)

**From**
- Stable traffic but declining checkout conversion
- Increasing cart abandonment at payment stage
- Pressure to “fix checkout fast”

**To**
- Restore and improve checkout conversion
- Reduce friction without harming trust
- Maintain average order value

**Who experiences this change the most**
- First-time buyers
- Mobile users
- Customer support handling failed checkout complaints

---

## 2. What do we believe — and what do we know?
(M → A)

**What we believed to be true**
- Checkout is too long
- Fewer steps will increase conversion
- Speed matters more than reassurance

**What evidence supported this**
- Funnel analytics showed drop-off at payment
- Internal benchmarks favored shorter checkouts
- Competitor checkouts appeared simpler

**What felt uncertain or risky**
- Whether trust signals were being removed
- Whether payment failures were perceived as risk
- Whether different user segments behaved differently

---

## 3. What does reality push back on?
(A — Align)

**How users behave today**
- Hesitate before payment
- Re-check delivery timelines and return policies
- Abandon and message support instead of retrying

**Constraints that became clear**
- New users lack brand trust
- Payment failures create anxiety
- Mobile users are more sensitive to ambiguity

**What changed after looking at reality**
- Speed alone wasn’t the issue
- Confidence and clarity mattered more at payment
- Removing steps removed reassurance

---

## 4. What is the next meaningful step?
(P — Proceed)

**Purpose of the next milestone**
- Increase checkout confidence, not just speed

**What we deliberately did not do**
- Full checkout redesign
- Aggressive discounting
- Forcing guest checkout

**Smallest step that proves direction**
- Add clear delivery dates before payment
- Surface return policy inline
- Improve payment failure messaging

**How we’ll know this worked**
- Reduced abandonment at payment
- Fewer checkout-related support tickets
- Stable or improved AOV

---

## 5. How will we know if we’re wrong?
(S — Sync)

**Signals we’ll monitor**
- Step-level conversion rate
- Payment retry success
- Support tickets tagged “checkout”

**Review cadence**
- Daily for first week, then weekly

**Reroute triggers**
- Conversion does not improve after 7 days
- Support tickets increase
- AOV drops significantly
