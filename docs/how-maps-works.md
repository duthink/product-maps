# How MAPS Works (and When It Breaks)

**Product MAPS** is the framework. **MAPS** is the four-step navigation loop inside it.

**MAPS stands for: Mark, Align, Proceed, Sync**

This document explains:
- how MAPS is meant to be used in real product work
- where teams commonly misuse it
- how to recognize drift early

If you’re new here, start with [README.md](../README.md), then come back.

---

## What MAPS is (and is not)

MAPS is designed to help teams:
- slow down thinking without slowing down delivery
- surface assumptions early
- make trade-offs explicit
- adapt without panic when things change

MAPS is **not**:
- a replacement for strategy
- a culture assessment
- a replacement for roadmaps, PRDs, or discovery
- a guarantee of success

Think of MAPS as a short navigation note your team keeps updating as the terrain changes.

---

## The core failure MAPS prevents

Most product failures don’t happen because teams chose the “wrong solution.”

They happen because:
- the starting point was misunderstood
- reality was ignored or oversimplified
- activity was mistaken for progress
- feedback arrived, but no one knew how to respond

MAPS exists to interrupt those patterns early—when change is still cheap.

---

## Why MAPS is cyclical

MAPS is not linear.

Teams revisit:
- **Mark** when assumptions change
- **Align** when new evidence appears
- **Proceed** when priorities shift or scope drifts
- **Sync** continuously

If you treat MAPS as “done once,” it stops working.

---

## Common ways MAPS breaks

Avoid these patterns:

- **Turning MAPS into a form everyone fills individually**  
  MAPS works best as a shared conversation. Use the map to capture what the group aligned on.

- **Over-documenting instead of deciding**  
  If the map grows but clarity doesn’t, stop. Decide what you need to decide, then move.

- **Using MAPS to justify a decision already made**  
  That’s storytelling, not navigation.

- **Treating MAPS as compliance**  
  If it feels mandatory, it will be ignored or gamed.

---

## What good MAPS usage looks like

In healthy teams, MAPS shows up as:
- shorter alignment discussions
- clearer trade-offs
- fewer surprises after launch
- faster recognition of “this isn’t working”
- safer conversations about changing direction

You won’t “feel” MAPS working.
You’ll notice confusion decreasing.

---

## A final reminder

MAPS does not replace judgment.
It supports it.

If MAPS ever feels heavy, simplify the map—not the thinking.
