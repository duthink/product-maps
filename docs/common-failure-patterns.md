# Common Product Failure Patterns MAPS Helps Expose

**Product MAPS** is the framework. **MAPS** is the four-step loop inside it (Mark, Align, Proceed, Sync).

MAPS does not prevent failure.
It helps teams see failure earlier—and respond while change is still possible.

Below are patterns MAPS frequently surfaces.

---

## 1) The imagined user problem

Teams build for a user that exists mostly in decks.

Symptoms:
- heavy reliance on personas
- little exposure to real usage
- surprise at basic resistance

MAPS surfaces this during:
- **Mark** (assumptions you’re making)
- **Align** (how users actually behave)

---

## 2) The feature accumulation trap

Progress is measured by:
- tickets closed
- features shipped
- roadmap velocity

But outcomes don’t move.

MAPS forces:
- purpose per milestone (**Proceed**)
- explicit non-goals (**Proceed**)

This often leads to less work with more impact.

---

## 3) The validation theater pattern

Teams “validate” by:
- quick surveys
- internal demos
- cherry-picked signals

Reality arrives late.

MAPS makes teams ask:
- what evidence actually changed our minds?
- what did we ignore?

---

## 4) The late panic pivot

Everything seems fine—until it isn’t.

Then:
- priorities change weekly
- trust erodes
- teams thrash

MAPS encourages:
- earlier sync points (**Sync**)
- explicit reroute criteria (**Sync**)

Pivots become adjustments, not emergencies.

---

## 5) The silent misalignment

Everyone agrees in meetings.
Execution tells a different story.

MAPS exposes:
- mismatched definitions of success (**Mark**)
- hidden constraints (**Align**)
- unspoken trade-offs (**Proceed**)

The Product Map becomes a neutral alignment surface.

---

## Final note

If MAPS feels uncomfortable, it often surfaced something real.

Treat discomfort as a signal—not a reason to skip the map.
