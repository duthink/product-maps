# How to Use Multiple Product Maps (Without Creating Chaos)

**Product MAPS** is the framework. **MAPS** is the four-step loop inside it.

**MAPS stands for: Mark, Align, Proceed, Sync**

As MAPS gets adopted, a common question comes up:

> Should we have one Product Map, or many?

Answer:
**Create one Product Map per meaningful decision scope.**

This document explains what that means in practice.

---

## Product Maps are about decisions (not org charts)

Product Maps do not belong to:
- departments
- roles
- reporting lines

They belong to **decisions**.

A Product Map exists to help a group of people navigate a shared set of assumptions, constraints, and outcomes.

If two groups are making **different decisions**, they need different maps.
If they are making the **same decision**, they should share one map.

---

## The three scopes that actually work

### 1) Company / product-level Product Map

Use a single shared Product Map when decisions affect:
- overall product direction
- positioning or value proposition
- major bets (new market, new platform, new model)
- shared assumptions about users and success

This map answers:
- What problem are we (as a company) solving?
- What reality are we betting on?
- What would make this direction wrong?

**Characteristics**
- changes slowly
- high-level
- referenced often
- owned by leadership, discussed widely

You should usually have **only one** of these.

---

### 2) Team / domain-level Product Maps

Create separate Product Maps when:
- assumptions differ
- constraints differ
- success signals differ

Examples:
- Growth → acquisition, activation, retention
- Operations / manufacturing → throughput, reliability, adoption
- Education / content → learning outcomes, engagement
- Support / CX → resolution speed, trust, escalation

These maps answer:
- What does success mean for this domain?
- What realities constrain us here?
- What signals matter most?

**Characteristics**
- more concrete
- updated more frequently
- can disagree with each other
- feed insights upward to the company/product-level map

Disagreement isn’t the problem.
Unspoken disagreement is.

---

### 3) Individual / role-level Product Maps (optional)

Sometimes individuals create Product Maps to:
- clarify thinking
- prepare for alignment meetings
- onboard into a new area

These maps are:
- short-lived
- rough
- personal

They’re most useful when they:
- surface assumptions early
- get merged into a shared map later

They should not become long-term sources of truth.

---

## How multiple Product Maps relate

Think of Product Maps as **nested, not hierarchical**:

- Company/product-level maps set direction
- Domain-level maps explore execution within constraints
- Individual maps support thinking, not authority

Information flows:
- **Upward** when domain realities challenge strategy
- **Downward** when direction or assumptions change

No map should silently override another.
If maps disagree, make the disagreement explicit and decide what changes.

---

## When you should NOT create a new Product Map

Avoid creating a new map when:
- the decision scope is already covered
- a map exists but feels uncomfortable
- you’re trying to justify a decision already made
- the work is low-risk and well understood

In those cases:
- update the existing map
- or skip MAPS entirely

More maps do not equal more clarity.

---

## A simple rule to remember

If you’re unsure whether to create another Product Map, ask:

- Are we making a different decision?
- Do we operate under different constraints?
- Do we measure success differently?

If yes → create a new map.  
If no → update the existing one.

## Final note

MAPS works when:
- maps are visible
- assumptions are explicit
- updates are normal

If Product Maps start multiplying without clarity, pause and revisit decision scope.

Navigation works best when everyone agrees which map they’re using.
